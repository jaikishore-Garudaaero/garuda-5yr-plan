The operating model

Management should steer the company, not chase the work

Today the founders and senior management spend most of their day on team management: following up on quotes, approving discounts, chasing deliveries and collections. Every decision queues at the top, approvals wait, and nobody below management owns a profit number. My proposal is to appoint a P&L Head for each division who owns sales, projects and delivery through to cash, with everything visible in the systems. Management then steps in only when a number or a process goes the wrong way.

Our 40-use-case catalogue (the SkyReach plan) is the prospecting list. Not every use case should be monetised the same way. 1 · Sell to existing operators Hardware + AMC

The customer already runs the service manually; our drone makes them faster and safer.

  • Waterproofing, coating and painting drone: the same platform sprays water repellent, primer or paint on facades after cleaning, so seepage repair needs no scaffolding. Sold to applicators and paint partners at ₹20–35 L.
  • Solar cleaning drone: washes dusty solar panels on rooftops and parks where robots cannot reach, restoring lost generation. Sold to O&M contractors at ₹8–15 L.
  • Fire reconnaissance kit: a thermal drone that is airborne in 90 seconds to show fire spread floor by floor and locate trapped people. Sold to fire services at ₹8–35 L.
  • Police and crowd kits: drones with loudspeaker and live video for festivals, rallies and patrol vehicles. Sold to police at ₹8–15 L.

2 · Rent out Rental pool

Needed in bursts; owning makes no sense for the customer, a rental pool earns well for us.

  • Structural audit and facade pre-survey kit: thermal and zoom flights map cracks, spalling and seepage on old buildings so engineers only send cradles where needed. Rented to structural engineers at ₹0.8–2 L a month.
  • Thermal inspection kit: finds hot spots in solar panels, electrical panels and roofs before they fail. ₹60k–1.5 L a month.
  • Event surveillance kit: aerial crowd view, loudspeaker and live feed for festivals and rallies. ₹2–4 L per event.
  • Construction survey and crane inspection kit: weekly progress maps and a 30-minute crane check instead of a 2.5-hour climb. ₹50k–1.2 L a month.
  • Disaster standby kit: thermal search, mapping and supply drop drones kept ready through the monsoon. Seasonal contract.

3 · Rent and sell (rent-to-own) Try, then buy

Customers start with rental or service and buy once payback is proven; rent paid counts toward the price.

  • High-rise facade cleaning: FM firms rent the cleaning drone for a season (₹1.5–2.5 L a month), see the savings on scaffolding and rope crews, then buy.
  • Apartment and campus security docks: a docked drone flies night perimeter patrols and responds to alarms; societies subscribe first, then buy with AMC.
  • Line inspection kits: utilities start with our inspection service on their power lines, then buy their own fleet and train crews with us.
  • Training drones: partner RPTOs and colleges lease training drones and simulators, then buy as batches grow.

4 · Garuda-operated service DaaS only

High-skill, high-liability or regulated work stays with our own crews.

  • Transmission line patrol: corridor flights with thermal and zoom cameras find hot joints, broken insulators and tree encroachment; AI ranks the defects. ₹2,000–5,000 per line-km.
  • Pilot-wire stringing: a heavy-lift drone flies the first rope across each tower span, avoiding shutdowns at road, rail and river crossings. ₹15–30 L per project.
  • Tethered high-rise fire suppression: a hose fed from a fire truck is lifted by drone to spray water at floors beyond ladder reach. R&D and controlled trials only.
  • Industrial stack and boiler inspection: caged and contact drones inspect chimneys, boilers and tanks without scaffolding or long shutdowns. Per inspection, partner-led.

Capacity check: is ₹500–700 cr possible for us?

Manufacturing: not the constraint

₹160 cr of agri revenue needs about 1,100 drones a year, plus a few hundred surveillance and specialised units. Our stated capacity is 15,000–25,000 a year.

People and channel: the real build

We need about 150 franchise partners and dealers, 15 partner RPTOs, ~300 field pilots and 6 P&L teams. RPTO's 5,000 pilots a year is roughly a fifth of India's annual training output, which is ambitious and depends on Plans 1–3.

Cash: the binding constraint

At ₹600 cr and today's ~415-day DSO, receivables would be about ₹680 cr. At 120 days they are about ₹200 cr, less than today. The plan only works if collections are fixed.

Performance pay · P&L share, team incentive and the Sales Run

Paid to win: a sales team that runs on results

P&L ownership works only if the people who own the number share in it. Each P&L Head receives 5% of their division's collected sales as the P&L share. The P&L Head decides, based on sales performance, whether to fund a kitty for the Sales Run in the last four months of the year; it may be given or it may not. On top of this, P&L Heads earn yearly shares when they cross three target slabs.

3 · The sales team: 1% of every sale, shared

How it is shared

The 1% of each collected sale is split among the people who won it: the closer, pre-sales and the delivery lead. The P&L Head sets the split rule at the start of the year.

What it means in practice

A team that collects ₹2 cr in a year shares ₹2 L in incentives. The P&L Head can add more from their share for outstanding performance.

Fair-play rules

Paid on cash collected, not on PO value. Reversed if the customer defaults within 12 months. No incentive on deals below the price floor.
